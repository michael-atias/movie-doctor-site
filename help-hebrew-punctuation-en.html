Hebrew subtitles with punctuation in the wrong place

Updated: 25 November 2026

The line shows its full stop or question mark on the wrong side. On the TV it looks one way, on the phone another, and on the PC a third.

Why it happens

Hebrew runs right to left, but punctuation marks are neutral: they belong to no direction, and the player decides where they go. Older subtitle tools used a trick: they physically moved the full stop to the start of the line in the file, so that one particular player would show it at the end. Every other player shows it reversed.

How to fix it

  1. Open the subtitle file in the free Subtitle Edit.
  2. Choose Tools, then Fix common errors.
  3. Tick the right-to-left (RTL) options and check in the preview that punctuation is back at the end of each line.
  4. Save the file as UTF-8.

The right way is punctuation at the end of the sentence in the file itself, with an invisible direction mark before each line telling the player it runs right to left. Then it reads right everywhere.

Related problem: gibberish instead of Hebrew

If you see characters like "ùìåí" instead of letters, the file is saved in an old encoding (Windows-1255). Open it in Subtitle Edit with that encoding and save it again as UTF-8.
