Subtitles too early or too late: how to fix them

Updated: 25 November 2026

You press play and the line appears a second before anyone speaks, or stays on screen after they finish. It is the most common subtitle problem, and when the offset is the same through the whole video it is easy to fix.

Why it happens

  • The subtitle file was made for a different version of the video, with a longer or shorter intro.
  • The video was cut or edited after the subtitles were made.
  • The subtitles were timed by hand and are off by a few seconds.

Quick fix while watching: VLC

  1. Open the video and the subtitles in VLC.
  2. Subtitles too early? Press H to delay them. Each press moves them 50 milliseconds.
  3. Subtitles too late? Press G to bring them forward.
  4. Keep pressing until each line appears as the sentence starts. VLC shows the offset in the corner.

The catch: the fix lasts only until you close the player. Next time, or on a TV or phone, the problem is back.

Permanent fix: save a new subtitle file

To make it work in every player, shift every time in the SRT file itself. The free Subtitle Edit does this: open the file, choose Synchronization, then Adjust all times, enter the offset you found in VLC, and save.

That works well when the offset is the same from start to end. If the subtitles are on time at the start and late by the middle, the offset changes, and you need the guide to subtitles that drift.
